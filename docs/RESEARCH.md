# Research

The problem here solves, who it is for, what already exists, and what is still not known. Every fact has a source and the date it was checked. Checked 6 November 2026.

## The problem

Regular absence is one of the clearest early signs that a child or learner needs help. Schools in England have to notice it: the Department for Education's guidance *Working together to improve school attendance* requires an electronic attendance register ([GOV.UK, 2026 edition](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6a4f9ec6a6586e258d371bd0/Working_together_to_improve_school_attendance_2026.pdf)), and schools follow up anyone persistently absent.

The official definition of persistent absence: a pupil "has an absence rate of 10% or more for at least 20 possible attendance sessions in the current academic year" ([Monitor your school attendance: user guide, DfE, updated 5 November 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/monitor-your-school-attendance-user-guide/monitor-your-school-attendance-user-guide)).

It is a growing problem in schools. The Centre for Social Justice counted 1,644,813 pupils persistently absent in summer 2024, up 78% on before the pandemic ([School Absence Tracker, March 2025](https://www.centreforsocialjustice.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/CSJ-Absence_Tracker_Mar_25.pdf)).

## Who it is for, and why them

Outside school, many children and adults learn in groups that have none of these systems: Saturday and supplementary schools, faith classes, tuition centres, youth and community groups, and adult learning.

- "There are between 3,000 and 5,000 supplementary schools in Britain" (Ives and Wyvill, 2008), quoted in [Saturdays for success, London Borough of Harrow](https://www.harrow.gov.uk/downloads/file/23705/saturdays-for-success-how-supplementary-education-can-support-pupils.pdf). This is an old estimate.
- The UK "has over 3000 supplementary schools supporting tens of thousands of children", with "no statutory body monitoring standards" ([John Lyon's Charity, on the National Resource Centre for Supplementary Education](https://www.jlc.london/grants/jlc-extra/national-resource-centre-for-supplementary-education-nrcse/)).
- There are "no official records" or large surveys of supplementary schools ([University of Essex ISER blog, 13 January 2026](https://www.iser.essex.ac.uk/blog/2026/01/13/exploring-supplementary-education-in-england-statistical-insights-and-research-opportunities)).

Many of these groups are run by volunteers and meet in halls and community centres. We expect most keep registers on paper or in a spreadsheet, but that is not yet verified (see the end of this page). That is where the job comes from: *when a session starts, I want to know who is here in seconds, so I can start teaching and know who to check on.*

## What exists, and the gap

| What people use | What it does well | What it misses for these groups |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Paper registers | Fast, familiar codes, no signal needed | Nobody adds it up, so patterns are noticed late or never |
| Spreadsheets and online forms | Free, can be shared | Slow on a phone, no warning when someone drifts, easy to lose when the volunteer leaves |
| School management systems | Built for statutory school registers | Built and priced for schools; heavy for a Saturday class |

The gap here fills: a register as fast as paper, that works with no signal, tells you who to check on, costs nothing, and keeps the data with the group. The table describes kinds of tool, not named products: no product comparison has been checked yet.

## What this means for the app

- The 90% line matches the official 10% absence threshold, so the app speaks the same language as schools and local authorities.
- **A known difference:** here flags anyone below 90% from their first few sessions, while the official measure needs at least 20 possible sessions. For a weekly Saturday class, 20 sessions is about half a year, so flagging earlier is deliberate here, but it should be shown in the app ("below 90% after 6 sessions") rather than left implied. Recorded as a decision to make, not changed yet.
- Working with no signal comes from where these groups meet, and is built in version 2.

## Not verified

- What supplementary schools and community groups actually use for registers today. No survey was found.
- How many such groups there are now: the best estimate is from 2008.
- Whether volunteers would use a phone app at all. This needs real users: the next step is to put here in front of three groups and watch them take a register.
- Named competing products and their prices.
